THE TRIAL

What they actually measured

They ran the trial everyone selling resin skips. Jung and colleagues took 58 healthy adults, split them, and had one group chew gum three times a day for six months while the other group did nothing. This is a randomised controlled trial out of Yonsei University College of Dentistry, not a before and after photo on a sales page.

Maximum bite force went up, and that is the number the resin crowd screenshots. But the researchers measured why. The contact area between the upper and lower teeth grew, so more teeth were touching and the bite landed harder. That is coordination, not size.

The two things people are actually chasing did not move. Masseter thickness, the jaw muscle itself: no significant change. Mandible shape, the bone: no change either. Six months, three sessions a day, and the face stayed the face.

"You trained the aim, not the size."

THE MECHANISM

A jawline is bone, muscle and fat

A jawline is three layers stacked. At the bottom is the mandible, your lower jaw bone. Over its back corner sits the masseter, the thick muscle that snaps your mouth shut. On top of both is a layer of fat and skin. The sharp line you see is the sum of all three.

The bone is the foundation, and its shape is set by genetics and growth. You are not remodelling a load bearing bone by chewing on it for a few months, and the trial confirmed it did not budge. The masseter can respond to work in principle, which is why nobody should claim it can never grow, but in the only randomised trial six months of chewing did not thicken it. The bite got stronger because the teeth met cleaner, the equivalent of a rifle sighting in rather than the barrel getting wider.

The layer that genuinely decides how sharp a jaw reads is the fat on top. Facial fat tracks body fat, and how heavy a face looks is read by other people as a health cue (Coetzee et al., Perception 2009). Drop body fat and the bone underneath shows more. No resin reaches that layer.

Meanwhile every hour of grinding firm resin loads the TMJ, the jaw joint that sits in a socket just in front of your ear with a small cartilage disc cushioning it. That disc is what takes the punishment when you treat your mouth like a gym, and it buys you no jawline for the trouble.

THE PROTOCOL

What really changes it

1
Judge the jaw by the fat, not the reps

The only layer you can move with your own effort is the fat sitting over the bone. Stop counting chews and start looking at total body fat, because facial fat follows it down.

2
Lower overall body fat

Facial adiposity tracks whole body adiposity, so a leaner body is what uncovers the bone line underneath. There is no spot reduction on a face and no chewing shortcut that touches this layer.

3
Keep chewing ordinary food

The trial showed a stronger, better aligned bite from normal chewing, which is genuinely useful. Eat normally and let the bite coordination happen, just do not expect a new face out of it.

4
Keep mastic in its real lane

Mastic resin has genuine oral health research behind it for plaque and breath, so it is not useless. Use it for that if you like, but drop the idea that firmer resin equals a sharper jaw.

5
Audit the joint, not the mirror

Pay attention to how the jaw joint feels, not just how the line looks. Clicking with pain, locking, or headaches after chewing are the signals that matter.

6
Run the NIDCR self care if it hurts

Stop the gum, put heat or cold on the joint, and do gentle jaw stretches. This is the US dental research institute's own first line for jaw joint trouble.

7
See a dentist if pain outlasts a few weeks

Self care handles a short flare. Pain that drags past a few weeks, or a jaw that locks, is a professional's job, not a resin's.

RED FLAGS

When to stop the gum

Gum alone is not going to shatter your jaw, and a 2025 study of 66 young adults (Yushchenko et al.) found no significant link between how much people chewed and jaw joint disorders. So this is not a panic. It is a line you do not cross.

The line is pain. An orofacial medicine specialist interviewed by Science News sees patients who chew firm gum into jaw pain, and others who clench so hard they grind through a night guard. If chewing is producing either of those, the resin is costing you more than it gives.

The specific signals: a jaw that clicks with pain, a jaw that locks, or chewing that brings on headaches. Any of those and the gum stops that day.

  • Stop the gum
  • Heat or cold on the joint
  • Gentle jaw stretches
  • See a dentist if pain lasts more than a few weeks

COMMON MISTAKES

Where people screw it up

✗
Reading the bite as the muscle

Your maximum bite force rising feels like proof the masseter is swelling. It is the teeth meeting cleaner over a bigger contact area, and the trial measured zero change in muscle thickness.

✗
Chewing firm resin for hours

Mastic is a tree resin from Chios, far harder than normal gum, and marathon sessions dump that load onto the TMJ disc. The specialist's clinic fills up with exactly this.

✗
Grinding through a night guard

Some people clench on resin so hard they wear through the guard meant to protect their teeth. That is force your jaw is absorbing, not a jawline it is building.

✗
Chasing the mandible

The bone shape is set by genetics and growth, and six months of daily chewing moved it exactly none. No amount of chewing remodels a bone you were born with.

✗
Ignoring a click that hurts

A painless click can be nothing, but clicking with pain, locking or headaches is the joint telling you to quit. Pushing through is how a self care case becomes a dentist case.

✗
Writing mastic off entirely

The attack is on the jawline claim, not the resin itself. Mastic still has real research for plaque and bad breath, so keep it for that and bin the face fantasy.

This guide is based on research, not medical advice. If your jaw clicks with pain, locks or brings on headaches, see a dentist about temporomandibular joint disorder.
