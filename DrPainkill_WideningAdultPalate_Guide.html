The Evidence

What the numbers actually say

The main trial is Brunetto and colleagues, published in Progress in Orthodontics in 2022. It was a prospective controlled trial in adults around 28 years old who had two things at once: a narrow upper jaw and diagnosed obstructive sleep apnea. Fourteen got MARPE, an expander anchored into the bone with mini implants. Ten got nothing and served as the control group.

The treated group went from 28.8 apnea events per hour to 11.5. The control group went from 27.4 to 24.2, which is basically noise. That gap matters, because it means the drop wasn't just people sleeping better on the second test night.

A second line of evidence comes from Stanford: Yoon, Liu, Guilleminault and colleagues, Sleep Medicine, 2020. They used DOME, an implant anchored expander plus small bone cuts, in adults averaging 30. Their apnea index fell from 17.7 to 8.2, roughly half, with less nasal blockage, more REM sleep and less daytime sleepiness.

"Same patients, same beds, same lungs. The only thing that changed was how wide the bone was."

  • Apnea events per hour: 28.8 to 11.5 in the MARPE group, a 65% average drop
  • 78.5% of treated patients at least halved their apnea
  • 35.7% dropped into the normal range, under 5 events per hour
  • Daytime sleepiness score fell from 12.9 to 7.3
  • Snoring time went from 15.7% of the night to 3.8%
  • Average oxygen rose from 91.9% to 94.3%
  • DOME (Stanford): apnea index 17.7 to 8.2, more REM, less nasal blockage

The Science

One bone, two jobs

The roof of your mouth and the floor of your nose are the same plate of bone, viewed from opposite sides. Think of a two story house where the ceiling of the ground floor is the floorboards of the room above. Narrow the house and both rooms shrink at once. You can't have a narrow, high arched palate and a roomy nasal floor at the same time. The geometry doesn't allow it.

That costs you twice. Upstairs, a narrow nasal floor raises nasal resistance, so breathing through your nose takes more effort and you drift toward mouth breathing. Downstairs, the tongue loses its parking space. It's built to rest up against the palate, but if the vault is narrow and pointed, there's nowhere for it to go. So it sits low and back, toward the throat. At night, when muscle tone drops, that low tongue falls back further into the airway. That's the collapse a sleep study counts as an apnea event.

Expansion attacks the bone directly. Your palate is two halves joined by a seam down the middle. An expander splits that seam and pushes the halves apart. The nasal floor widens, so air moves through the nose with less resistance. The palate widens, so the tongue gets room to sit up and forward instead of slumping into your airway. The trial outcomes line up with both effects: less nasal blockage in DOME, and far less snoring and higher average oxygen in MARPE.

Here's why adults need the heavy machinery. In kids the seam hasn't fused, so a standard expander pressing on the teeth can open it. In adults the seam has fused. Push on the teeth and the teeth tip outward while the bone stays put. That's crooked teeth, no wider airway. MARPE gets around this by anchoring mini implants into the palate bone itself, so the force goes straight to the skeleton. DOME adds small bone cuts to help the seam give. In some older adults the seam won't split without surgery at all.

"Your tongue isn't lazy. It's homeless, and at night it moves into your throat."

The Options

MARPE vs DOME vs surgery

All three do the same basic job: split the fused seam and widen the upper jaw. What separates them is how much help the bone needs to give way. Which one fits you is a call your specialist makes from imaging of the seam, not something you pick from a menu.

MARPE is the one with the controlled trial behind it. DOME has good numbers too, but they come from case series without a control group. Surgical expansion exists for the people whose seam has fused too solidly to split any other way.

The Split

Is your palate the problem

Expansion only makes sense when the apnea is tied to a narrow upper jaw and nose floor. Sleep apnea has plenty of other drivers, and widening a palate that was never the bottleneck fixes nothing.

The Other Side

The honest limits

The studies are small. Brunetto had 24 people, wasn't randomized, followed them for 6 months, and used a home sleep test. The DOME work is case series without a control group. "Cut in half" is the average result in small studies, not a promise for your airway.

Most people weren't cured. About a third reached the normal range in the best trial. The rest improved but still had apnea, which is exactly why CPAP stays the standard and expansion stays a specialist option.

It has real costs. Mini implants can inflame the gums or loosen. Five were lost in the Brunetto trial without wrecking the result, but you should expect that it can happen. You'll need braces afterward to sort out your bite, and in older adults the seam may not split without surgery.

"A 65% drop in a small group is a reason to ask the question. It isn't a reason to skip the answer."

The Protocol

How to find out if this is you

1
Get a sleep study first

If you snore, gasp, or wake up tired, book a sleep study before anything else. Every number in this guide was measured that way, and without your own baseline you can't know whether you have apnea, how bad it is, or whether anything later helped.

2
Check yourself for palate signs

Look for a crossbite, crowded upper teeth, a high arched palate, or a nose that is always blocked, and write down which ones you have. These are the signs that point toward a narrow upper jaw, the only kind of apnea expansion was tested on.

3
Bring in two specialists

Take your sleep study and your list to a sleep doctor and to an orthodontist or oral surgeon who treats airway cases. You need both, because the sleep doctor judges what is driving your apnea and the jaw specialist judges whether your bone can be widened.

4
Ask for a CBCT of the suture

Ask whether they will image the midline seam of your palate with a CBCT scan. How fused that seam is decides whether MARPE alone can split it or whether you need bone cuts or surgery.

5
Ask which method and why

Ask straight out whether they recommend MARPE, DOME, or surgical expansion for you, and what in your scan drove that choice. A specialist who plans to use a teeth only expander on an adult jaw is planning to tip your teeth, not widen your bone.

6
Get the full plan before you start

Ask how long treatment runs, whether braces follow and for how long, and what the risks are, including gum inflammation and loose implants. You want all of this on the table before the first implant goes in, not halfway through.

7
Keep your CPAP the whole time

If you're on CPAP, keep using it every night through treatment. Expansion is not a replacement you choose yourself, and only your sleep doctor decides if and when that changes.

8
Repeat the sleep study after

Ask for a sleep study after treatment so you can compare it against your baseline. That comparison is the only real proof of whether the airway changed, and it's the number your sleep doctor needs before touching your CPAP plan.

Is It Working

What to watch

The outcomes that moved in the trials are the ones to track: snoring, daytime sleepiness, how easily you breathe through your nose, and the event count on a repeat sleep study. A bed partner who stops elbowing you is data. The repeat sleep study is proof.

Red, swollen gums around an implant, or an implant that feels loose, means call your provider. Don't wait it out and don't fiddle with it. Loose implants happened in the trial and were manageable when the team handled them.

Common Mistakes

Don't screw up the bone

✗
Ditching CPAP to wait for an expander

People hear 'cut in half' and stop using their machine early. That leaves them with untreated apnea every night while they wait on a treatment that doesn't fully fix most people.

✗
Skipping the sleep study

Some go straight to an orthodontist because they snore. Without a diagnosis you might not have apnea at all, or you might have a kind expansion does nothing for.

✗
Assuming every apnea is a jaw problem

Weight, a large tongue, a floppy soft palate, or a set back jaw can all drive apnea. Widen a palate that was never the bottleneck and you take on implants and braces for nothing.

✗
Letting someone use a kid's expander on an adult

A teeth only expander can't split a fused adult seam. You end up with tipped teeth and the same narrow airway.

✗
Expecting a cure

In the best trial about a third reached normal, and most improved without getting there. Go in expecting a cure and a solid partial result will feel like a failure when it isn't one.

✗
Ignoring gum trouble or skipping braces

Inflamed gums and loose implants need your provider's attention, not a wait and see approach. Skipping the braces afterward leaves your bite sorting itself out badly after the jaw has been widened.

This guide is based on published research and is not medical advice. Sleep apnea needs a diagnosis from a sleep doctor, and palatal expansion needs evaluation by a qualified orthodontist or oral surgeon. Do not stop CPAP or start any treatment without them.
