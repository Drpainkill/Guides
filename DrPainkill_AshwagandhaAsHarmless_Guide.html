THE BAN AND THE LIVERS

Natural and banned can both be true

In 2023 Denmark pulled ashwagandha from food supplements, the first country in the EU to do it. The move followed a 2020 risk assessment by the Technical University of Denmark, which concluded the root and root extract can push on thyroid hormones and sex hormones, raised a concern that it may induce abortion (drawn from traditional use and animal data, not human cases), and said it could not set a safe lower intake. Notice what is not on that list: liver deaths. The ban was about hormones and the lack of a known safe dose. Some scientists have since criticized the assessment for methodological gaps, and most countries still sell it freely.

The liver problem comes from a separate thread of evidence. Björnsson and colleagues documented five cases of ashwagandha induced liver injury, three in Iceland and two through the US Drug Induced Liver Injury Network. Every patient went jaundiced. A larger series from India then tracked 23 cases, and that is where the story turns serious.

This is why the video stayed two sided, and why this guide does too. The trial data for stress and testosterone is real. The liver reaction is rare. Neither fact cancels the other.

"A whole country banned it, and not one of the reasons was the thing that actually kills."

  • Björnsson 2020: five cases, mean age 43, all developed jaundice plus nausea, tiredness, itching and belly discomfort
  • Onset landed 2 to 12 weeks after starting, and the itch and jaundice lasted 5 to 20 weeks
  • No liver failure in that series: tests normalized within 1 to 5 months of stopping
  • Chemical analysis confirmed ashwagandha and found no other toxins, so the herb itself was the cause
  • Philips 2023 (India): of 8 single ingredient cases checked closely, 5 already had chronic liver disease, 3 tipped into acute on chronic failure and all 3 died

THE MECHANISM

A rare, delayed bile jam

Your liver makes bile and pushes it out through ducts as thin as thread. In a small, unpredictable set of people, ashwagandha stalls that flow. The injury is cholestatic, which means bile stops draining properly instead of the liver cells being burned down directly. The plumbing clogs rather than the factory burning.

When bile cannot drain, bilirubin backs up. Bilirubin is the yellow pigment bile carries out of the body, so once it floods the blood your eyes and skin go yellow, your urine goes dark, and bile salts in the skin set off a deep, relentless itch. Pale stools follow, because the pigment that normally colors them never reaches the gut.

The cruel part is the timing. This shows up 2 to 12 weeks after you start, long after anyone would connect it to a capsule they have been swallowing without incident. By then you are blaming a bad meal or a rough week, not the supplement.

In a healthy liver the jam usually clears within 1 to 5 months once you stop. In a liver that is already damaged, the same back up can tip the whole organ into failure. That is the exact split between the Iceland and US cases, who recovered, and the three Indian patients with existing liver disease, who did not.

THE SAFETY CHECKLIST

How to take it without getting ambushed

This is the part that did not fit in sixty seconds. If you are going to take it, take it watching for the one reaction nobody prints on the tub.

1
Memorize the five warning signs

Yellow eyes, dark urine, itching, pale stools, nausea. These are the fingerprint of a bile jam, and the itch often comes first. Treat any one of them as a reason to act, not wait.

2
Mark the 2 to 12 week window

The reaction is sneaky because it is delayed, hitting weeks after you start when you have stopped thinking about the capsule. Stay alert through that whole stretch, not just the first few days.

3
Stop at the first sign, then get tested

If any warning sign shows, stop taking it that day and get a liver panel from your doctor. In healthy livers the injury cleared in 1 to 5 months after stopping, so the stopping is the treatment.

4
Skip it entirely if you are higher risk

Do not touch it if you have liver disease, are pregnant, or have a thyroid condition. The three deaths were all in people with existing liver disease, and the Danish flags were thyroid, sex hormones and a possible abortion effect.

5
Do not stack it with other liver load

Four of the five Björnsson patients were also taking other supplements. Keep it away from turmeric extract, green tea extract, garcinia, black cohosh and red yeast rice, the other five botanicals most reported in US herbal liver injury.

6
Tell your doctor you take it

To your liver this is a drug, not a harmless herb, and chemical analysis confirmed the herb itself caused the injury with no contamination to blame. Say it out loud at appointments so any jaundice gets connected to the right cause fast.

7
Be honest about what you are buying

The stress and cortisol drop are real and the salivary testosterone rose 14.7% over placebo. But in that same trial the men did not actually feel more energetic, so weigh a modest, partly subjective benefit against a rare liver gamble.

COMMON MISTAKES

Where people get blindsided

✗
Blaming everything but the capsule

Because the injury lands 2 to 12 weeks in, people pin the jaundice on a bad meal, alcohol or stress. That delay is exactly what lets the bile keep backing up unchecked.

✗
Treating natural as a safety guarantee

A whole EU country pulled it in 2023 and it still ends up in the top six botanicals for US liver injury. Plant origin tells you nothing about what it does to your bile ducts.

✗
Taking it on a loaded supplement shelf

Stacking it with turmeric, green tea extract or other liver taxing botanicals multiplies the load on one organ. Most of the documented liver patients were on other supplements at the same time.

✗
Taking it with a damaged liver

The only deaths happened in people who already had chronic liver disease and tipped into failure. A liver with no reserve cannot ride out a bile jam the way a healthy one does.

✗
Sitting on the first itch

A deep, relentless itch is often the opening symptom, and people wait for it to pass. Waiting lets bilirubin keep climbing when stopping is what reverses it.

✗
Chasing a feeling the trial never promised

The 14.7% was a salivary testosterone number, not a reported surge in energy or drive. Expecting to feel transformed sets you up to keep upping and extending it for nothing.

This guide is based on research, not medical advice. See a doctor or hepatologist about any liver symptoms, and about pregnancy or thyroid conditions, before taking ashwagandha.
