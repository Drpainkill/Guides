THE STUDY

Where the scare actually started

The whole myth traces back to one paper: van der Merwe, Brooks and Myburgh, Clinical Journal of Sport Medicine, 2009. Twenty college rugby players in South Africa, double blind, placebo controlled, crossover design. That is a respectable setup. The problem is what it set out to measure, and what people later claimed it measured.

The dosing was not what most lifters take. Players did a loading phase of 25 g of creatine a day for 7 days, then dropped to 5 g a day for 14 days. Three weeks total. During loading, DHT (dihydrotestosterone) rose 56%. During maintenance it settled to about 40% above baseline.

Here is the detail that never makes the meme: testosterone itself did not change. What shifted was the DHT to testosterone ratio. So the study showed that, under a heavy loading dose, more of the hormone picture tilted toward DHT for a few weeks in 20 athletes. That is a real hormone finding. It is not a hair finding.

The study measured DHT accurately. It was not fake and it was not wrong. It simply never looked at a single follicle, used a dose four to five times the normal daily amount during loading, had 20 people, and was never replicated.

"A hormone went up in 20 rugby players for three weeks. The internet heard bald spots."

  • DHT rose 56% during the 25 g a day loading week
  • DHT stayed about 40% above baseline during 5 g a day maintenance
  • Testosterone did not change; the DHT to testosterone ratio rose
  • No hair counts, no density, no follicle measurements of any kind
  • Never replicated by any later study

THE SCIENCE

How hair really goes

Male pattern hair loss is mostly genetic. Some of the follicles on your scalp are built sensitive to DHT, and some are not. Your genes decide which follicles react. DHT is the trigger. Think of it as a loaded gun: the genes load it, DHT pulls it. No load, and the trigger does nothing.

In a sensitive follicle, DHT drives a slow shrink called miniaturization. It does not happen over a weekend. Over years, the follicle gets smaller and the hair it produces comes in thinner and shorter, until a thick terminal hair is reduced to something that barely shows. That is why a receding hairline creeps rather than collapses.

So the statement "DHT matters for hair loss" is true. The statement "creatine raises DHT enough to cause hair loss" has no evidence behind it. Those are two different claims, and the myth survives by blurring them. A blood hormone moving for three weeks tells you nothing about whether follicles shrank, because nobody looked at the follicles.

That gap is exactly what Lak and colleagues closed in 2025. Forty five resistance trained men took 5 g of creatine a day, no loading, or placebo, for 12 weeks. It was the first trial to examine the hair directly alongside the blood. Compared with placebo there was no difference in DHT, no difference in the DHT to testosterone ratio, and no difference in hair density, hair thickness or follicle health. Kidney markers (creatinine and eGFR) were also unchanged.

"Your genes load the gun. DHT pulls the trigger. Creatine was never shown to be holding it."

THE SPLIT

The scare study vs the hair study

Line the two papers up side by side and the myth falls apart on design alone. One measured a hormone under a heavy load. The other measured the hormone and the hair at the dose people actually take, for four times as long, in more than twice as many people.

THE PROTOCOL

How to take it without the panic

This protocol sticks to what was actually tested. Nothing here is designed to protect or regrow hair, because nothing in the evidence says creatine does either. It is about dosing the way the hair trial dosed, and putting the worry where it belongs.

1
Take 5 g a day

One 5 g serving daily is the dose the 2025 trial tested against placebo. At that dose, DHT, the DHT to testosterone ratio and every hair measure matched placebo over 12 weeks.

2
Skip the loading phase

Do not run 25 g a day for a week. That loading dose is the only condition where DHT was seen rising, and the 5 g no loading approach is the one with hair data behind it.

3
Keep it daily

The trial gave creatine every day for 12 weeks, so consistent daily use is the pattern the evidence actually covers. Stopping and restarting in loading bursts puts you back in the dosing pattern from the 2009 study.

4
Do not flush the tub over a hairline change

If your hairline moves, the evidence points at genetics and DHT sensitivity, not the scoop. Ditching creatine fixes nothing the data says creatine caused, and it hands you a false answer.

5
Look at your family

Male pattern hair loss is mostly genetic, so the men in your family are a better clue than your supplement shelf. If they thinned, your follicles may carry the same DHT sensitivity.

6
See a dermatologist if you are thinning

If you are already losing hair and worried, that is a job for a dermatologist, not a supplement audit. They can assess what is actually happening at the follicle instead of you guessing from a mirror.

THE OTHER SIDE

What the skeptics get right

The strongest honest pushback: the reassuring trial is one trial, with 45 men, over 12 weeks. That is not forever, and it is not every possible person. Nobody can say creatine is proven safe for every scalp for life, and this guide will not say it.

The skeptics are also right that DHT genuinely matters for hair loss. That part of the worry was never stupid. What was missing was any evidence that creatine pushes DHT high enough, for long enough, to shrink follicles. The one study that showed a rise used a 25 g loading dose and never looked at hair.

So the fair position is this. The best trial we have, at the normal 5 g dose, found no effect on DHT or hair over 12 weeks. The study behind the scare measured a hormone, not hair, and nobody ever repeated it. If your hair is going, that is genetics and DHT sensitivity, and the person to see is a dermatologist.

"One good trial is not forever. It is still one more hair count than the scare ever had."

COMMON MISTAKES

Do not waste the scoop

✗
Treating the 2009 study as a hair study

People quote it as proof creatine causes balding, when it never measured a single hair. They end up making decisions off a result the study never produced.

✗
Loading at 25 g because a forum said so

Lifters copy the loading phase without knowing it is the exact condition where DHT rose. They take on the only dose linked to the scare when the 5 g dose has hair data behind it.

✗
Blaming the tub for a receding hairline

The hairline moves, the creatine goes in the bin, and genetics gets a free pass. They lose the supplement and keep the actual cause, untreated.

✗
Expecting creatine to protect your hair

No difference from placebo means no harm found, not a hair benefit. Anyone hoping it prevents or reverses hair loss is reading something into the trial that is not there.

✗
Calling the 2009 study fake

It measured DHT accurately; it just measured the wrong thing for the hair question. Dismissing it as fake makes you easy to argue with and misses the real lesson about what it did and did not test.

✗
Skipping the dermatologist

Worried men burn months swapping supplements instead of getting the scalp looked at. Miniaturization works over years, and guessing does nothing to slow it.

This guide is based on published research and is not medical advice. If you are experiencing hair loss or have kidney concerns, see a dermatologist or your doctor.
