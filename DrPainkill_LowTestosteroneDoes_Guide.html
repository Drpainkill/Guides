The Problem

One hormone, every tissue

Testosterone is made in the testicles, dumped into the blood and read by receptors in muscle, bone, bone marrow, brain, fat and the penis. One source, six readers. That's why a shortfall never shows up as one clean symptom. It shows up as a scatter of small failures across the whole body, and each one gets blamed on something else.

The European Male Ageing Study (Wu et al., NEJM 2010) went through the whole list of symptoms men reported and asked which ones actually tracked with low testosterone. Most were noise. Three stood out, and all three are sexual. The penis is the most sensitive gauge in the system, so it breaks first and it breaks most reliably.

So how do you prove what low testosterone does? You run the experiment backwards. Take men who are genuinely low, restore the hormone, and see what comes back. That's exactly what the Testosterone Trials did, and the results are the map below.

"The bedroom isn't the whole problem. It's just the first organ to file a complaint."

  • Fewer morning erections: one of the three signs most tied to low T (EMAS, NEJM 2010)
  • Low sexual desire: the second of the three
  • Erectile dysfunction: the third
  • Testosterone Trials (Snyder et al., NEJM 2016): 790 men aged 65 or older with testosterone under 275 ng/dL
  • One year of treatment in those men increased sexual activity, sexual desire and erectile function

The Science

What restoring it actually fixed

Think of testosterone as a radio broadcast and every tissue as a receiver tuned to the same station. Turn the signal down and the receivers don't all fail at once. They each go quiet in their own way: marrow builds less, muscle builds less, the brain's mood tone dips. The trial results below are what happened when researchers turned the signal back up in older men who were low.

Blood is the cleanest example. Testosterone drives red blood cell production in the bone marrow. In the Anemia Trial (Roy et al., JAMA Internal Medicine 2017), older men with anemia nobody could explain got testosterone or placebo. Treatment corrected the anemia in 58% versus 22%. Fewer red cells means less oxygen hauled per heartbeat, which is a physical reason to feel flat, not a character flaw.

Bone and muscle follow the same logic. In the Bone Trial (Snyder et al., JAMA Internal Medicine 2017), a year of treatment raised volumetric trabecular bone density in the spine by 7.5% versus 0.8% on placebo. Testosterone also signals muscle to build protein, and across the Testosterone Trials 20.5% of treated men improved their 6 minute walk by 50 metres or more, versus 12.6% on placebo.

Then the fat loop. Belly fat carries an enzyme called aromatase, which converts testosterone into estradiol. Estradiol tells the brain to turn testosterone production down, and lower testosterone makes it easier to store more fat. It's a thermostat being fed bad readings by the room it's supposed to heat. In T4DM (Wittert et al., Lancet Diabetes and Endocrinology 2021), men aged 50 and over with a waist of 95 cm or more and prediabetes or new diabetes all did the same lifestyle program. After two years, 12.4% of the testosterone group had type 2 diabetes versus 21% on placebo, a 40% lower rate.

Now the honest part. Mood improved slightly and depressive symptoms dropped, but the vitality and fatigue score did not improve at all. And every number here comes from older men (65 plus in the Testosterone Trials, 50 plus in T4DM). They show what the hormone does in the body. They are not a promise for a 25 year old.

The Split

Loud signs vs specific signs

The symptoms everyone googles are the worst ones to go on. Tiredness, low mood and weight gain each have a dozen causes, and low testosterone is only one of them. The signs that actually point at the hormone are the boring clinical ones men are least likely to mention to a doctor.

The Checklist

Run the signs, head to toe

Go system by system and be honest. The first three items carry the most weight. Everything below them only means something when it stacks up alongside them, and none of it diagnoses anything on its own. That's what the blood test is for.

Write down what you tick. A doctor can work with a list. They can't work with "I just feel off."

"A checklist tells you to get tested. Only the blood tells you what's wrong."

  • Sexual (most specific): fewer morning erections than you used to have
  • Sexual (most specific): low sexual desire
  • Sexual (most specific): erections that fail
  • Mood and brain: low mood or more depressive symptoms
  • Energy and blood: anemia on a blood test with no explanation found
  • Bone: low bone density on a scan
  • Belly fat and blood sugar: a growing waist, prediabetes or rising blood sugar
  • Muscle: weaker, slower, less able to get around than you were

The Protocol

Test it, don't guess it

This is the order that gets you a real answer instead of a vibe. Steps 1 to 5 are the diagnosis. Steps 6 and 7 attack what suppresses testosterone, whatever the result. Step 8 only applies if the numbers say so.

1
Score the checklist first

Go through the list above and mark every item, starting with the three sexual signs. Those three are what the European Male Ageing Study found most tied to low testosterone, so they carry the most weight when you walk into the appointment.

2
Name the other suspects

Tell the doctor about your sleep, your mood and your drinking up front. Bad sleep, depression, thyroid problems and alcohol produce the same tiredness and weight gain, and they need ruling out, not hiding.

3
Book two early morning tests

Ask for total testosterone, drawn early in the morning, on two separate days. That's the American Urological Association standard (2018), because one reading is a snapshot and two is a confirmation.

4
Read the number against the cutoff

Under 300 ng/dL plus symptoms is low by the AUA definition, and the Endocrine Society puts its lower limit at 264 ng/dL. A low number without symptoms, or symptoms with a normal number, is not the diagnosis: you need both.

5
If it's low, ask for LH and FSH

These two hormones are the signal the brain sends to the testicles. The guidelines add them to tell whether the brain has stopped asking or the testicles have stopped answering, which are two different problems.

6
Kill the short nights

Stop running on 5 hour nights. One week of them cut daytime testosterone 10 to 15% in young men (Leproult and Van Cauter, JAMA 2011), so a sleep debt can drag your number down before you ever get to the clinic.

7
Shrink the belly

Losing belly fat removes aromatase tissue, which means less testosterone converted to estradiol and less signal telling your brain to turn production down. It's the one move that breaks the fat loop from the fat side.

8
If it's truly low, treat it properly

Confirmed low testosterone is a medical condition, and treatment from a doctor is real medicine that measurably fixed things in these trials. Let them choose whether and how to treat, and stay under their follow up.

The Other Side

What the trials don't say

The strongest counterargument is about who was studied. The Testosterone Trials enrolled men 65 and older, and T4DM enrolled men 50 and older with big waists and blood sugar problems. Those numbers tell you what the hormone does in bodies that need it. They say nothing about what happens when a young man with normal levels chases a higher one.

The skeptics are also right that the vague symptoms get oversold. In the same trial where sexual function and mood improved, the vitality and fatigue score didn't budge. If tiredness is your only complaint, low testosterone may not be your answer, and that's exactly why the protocol starts with a test instead of a conclusion.

"Real deficiency deserves real treatment. A hunch deserves a blood test."

Common Mistakes

How men waste the diagnosis

✗
Blaming your age

Men write off fading morning erections and desire as getting older and never mention them. Those are the three most specific signs on the list, and skipping them leaves the doctor guessing.

✗
Diagnosing yourself from tiredness

Tired, flat and heavier gets read as low T by default. It has a dozen other causes, and the trials showed no fatigue improvement anyway, so you can chase the wrong fix for months.

✗
Trusting one test

A single result gets treated as the final answer. The guideline calls for two early morning tests on separate days because one reading isn't a confirmation.

✗
Testing on a week of bad sleep

Men get bloods drawn in the middle of a 5 hour night stretch. Short sleep alone cut testosterone 10 to 15% in young men, so the number you get may be your sleep debt talking.

✗
Stopping at the testosterone number

A low result gets treated as the whole story. Without LH and FSH you don't know whether the brain or the testicles are the problem, and that changes what happens next.

✗
Copying older men's results

The 58%, 7.5% and 40% figures get quoted as if they apply to any man who wants more energy. They come from older men with low testosterone, and applying them to a 25 year old is reading someone else's lab report.

This guide is based on published research and is not medical advice. If you think you have low testosterone, see a doctor (a GP, urologist or endocrinologist) for proper testing and treatment.
